60 interview questions for household help

Good interviews ask for real examples, not promises. Pick ten questions — the general set plus the role you're hiring for — and score every candidate the same way.

By the Khadim editorial team. Last checked 29 October 2026. 4 min read.

How to use this guide

  • Choose 4–5 general questions and 5–6 role questions. Thirty minutes is enough.
  • Ask for examples: "Tell me about a time…" beats "Can you…?"
  • Use the scorecard at the end so you compare candidates fairly.
  • Follow with a paid trial and reference calls before you decide.

General questions for every role

  1. Tell me about your last job. What did a normal day look like?
  2. How long did you work there, and why did you leave?
  3. What part of this work do you enjoy most? What do you find hardest?
  4. Tell me about a time something went wrong at work. What did you do?
  5. How do you like to be told when an employer wants something done differently?
  6. What hours, days off and pay are you expecting?
  7. Is there anything you won't do, or can't do for health reasons?
  8. How would you get here each day, and how long does it take?
  9. Who can I speak to as a reference?
  10. What questions do you have for me?

Housekeeper

  1. Walk me through how you'd clean this kitchen from start to finish.
  2. Which cleaning products do you use on marble, wood and glass?
  3. How do you handle delicate clothes or fabrics you haven't seen before?
  4. If you break something valuable, what do you do?
  5. How do you plan a week's work so nothing is missed?
  6. What do you do when you run out of supplies?
  7. Have you looked after a home with pets? What did that involve?
  8. How do you iron a shirt? (Ask them to show you in the trial.)
  9. How do you keep a bathroom hygienic, not just clean-looking?
  10. What would you do if a stranger came to the door while we were out?

Cook

  1. Which cuisines do you cook well? What's your best dish?
  2. Have you cooked for special diets — diabetic, low-salt, Jain, vegan, allergies?
  3. How do you plan a week's meals and the grocery list?
  4. How do you store cooked food and leftovers safely?
  5. How do you keep the kitchen clean while you cook?
  6. How do you adjust spice and oil for children or older people?
  7. How do you cook for guests at short notice?
  8. If you shop for us, how do you keep track of spending?
  9. How do you check that vegetables and meat are fresh?
  10. Would you cook a trial meal for us? (Always pay for it.)

Nanny

  1. What ages have you cared for, and for how long?
  2. How do you put a baby down to sleep safely?
  3. What would you do if my child choked?
  4. How do you calm a child who's crying and won't stop?
  5. How do you handle tantrums or hitting?
  6. What does a good day for a two-year-old look like to you?
  7. How do you handle screen time and snacks?
  8. What would you do if my child had a high fever and you couldn't reach me?
  9. Tell me about a family you worked with whose rules were different from yours.
  10. How do you keep us updated during the day?

Elder carer

  1. What experience do you have caring for older people? Any with dementia?
  2. How would you help someone stand up from a chair safely?
  3. What would you do if my parent fell?
  4. How do you make sure medicines are taken at the right time?
  5. How do you help with bathing while respecting their dignity?
  6. What would you do if my parent refused to eat or take medicine?
  7. How do you spot signs that someone is unwell or getting worse?
  8. How do you keep someone company who's lonely or low?
  9. Are you comfortable with night duties? How do you stay alert?
  10. How do you look after yourself in a demanding care job?

Driver

  1. What licence do you hold, and for how many years?
  2. Have you had any accidents or traffic fines? What happened?
  3. How well do you know the routes to our children's school and my office?
  4. How do you drive differently with children or older passengers in the car?
  5. What basic car maintenance do you do?
  6. What would you do if the car broke down with the children inside?
  7. How do you handle long waiting times?
  8. Do you use your phone while driving?
  9. How do you keep a log of fuel and expenses?
  10. Are you comfortable with early mornings, late nights or out-of-town trips?

Scorecard

Score each area from 1 (weak) to 5 (excellent) for every candidate. Print this table, or copy it into a notebook.

AreaWhat good looks likeScore
ExperienceRelevant work, explained with specific examples/5
SkillsShows, not just tells, in the trial/5
SafetyKnows what to do in an emergency/5
CommunicationClear, honest, asks questions/5
ReliabilitySteady work history, good references/5
FitComfortable with your home, hours and routines/5

Questions you should not ask

Depending on where you live, some questions may be unlawful, and all of them are unfair: religion, caste, marriage or pregnancy plans, sexual orientation, political views, or health conditions unrelated to the job. Ask instead about what the job requires — "This role includes lifting; is that something you can do?"
