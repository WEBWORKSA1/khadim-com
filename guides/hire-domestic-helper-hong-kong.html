Hiring a foreign domestic helper in Hong Kong

What you must pay, the contract you must use, and the rest days and holidays your helper is entitled to — updated for the October 2026 wage increase.

By the Khadim editorial team. Last checked 29 October 2026. 3 min read.

Key facts

  • The Minimum Allowable Wage (MAW) is HK$5,220 a month for contracts signed on or after 3 October 2026, up from HK$5,100.
  • Employers must provide free food or a food allowance of at least HK$1,236 a month.
  • Helpers are employed on the government's two-year Standard Employment Contract and must live in.
  • Helpers are entitled to a weekly rest day and statutory holidays; statutory holidays cannot be paid off instead of taken.
  • Employment agencies may not charge job seekers more than 10% of their first month's wages.

1. The new minimum wage

The government reviews the MAW regularly. For employment contracts signed on or after 3 October 2026, the minimum is HK$5,220 a month — a rise of 2.35%. Contracts signed at the old rate of HK$5,100 on or before 2 October 2026 are still processed if submitted to the Immigration Department by 30 October 2026. The MAW is a floor: experienced helpers, especially those caring for babies or older people, are often paid more.

2. Food, accommodation and other costs

  • Food: provide free food, or a food allowance of at least HK$1,236 a month.
  • Accommodation: helpers must live in your home, with suitable accommodation and reasonable privacy.
  • Insurance: employees' compensation insurance is compulsory; most employers also buy medical cover.
  • Travel and visa: the employer pays the visa fee and travel to and from Hong Kong.
  • Agency fees: agencies may charge employers a service fee; they may charge the helper no more than 10% of the first month's wages.

3. The Standard Employment Contract

Every foreign domestic helper is employed on the government's Standard Employment Contract, which runs for two years and sets out wages, food, accommodation, duties, rest days and termination. You can add a schedule of duties and house rules, but nothing can reduce what the contract and law provide. Our contract builder works well as a side letter for house rules and the weekly schedule.

4. Rest days and holidays

Helpers are entitled to at least one rest day in every seven days, plus statutory holidays and paid annual leave under the Employment Ordinance. Statutory holidays must be taken — they can't be replaced with extra pay. If you need help on a holiday, agree an alternative day off in advance.

5. Duties

Helpers may only perform domestic duties in the employer's home listed in the contract. They may not be required to work for anyone else, or outside the home in a business. Driving generally needs specific approval; if you need a driver, hire one separately.

6. Ending a contract fairly

Either side can end the contract with one month's notice or a month's wages in lieu. On termination, pay all wages, untaken leave, any long service or severance payment due, and the return ticket. Notify the Immigration Department within seven days.

7. Good practice

  • Respect the rest day: no chores on the way out, and no curfew on a day off.
  • Give a private room or partitioned space, not a shared space with children.
  • Pay on time by bank transfer and keep receipts.
  • Let the helper keep their passport and phone.
  • Sign the Fair Home pledge.
