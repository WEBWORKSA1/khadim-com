Hiring a housemaid in the UAE

The legal routes, what it really costs, and the rights every domestic worker has under the UAE's 2022 domestic worker law — explained in plain English.

By the Khadim editorial team. Last checked 29 October 2026. 5 min read.

Key facts

  • Domestic work is governed by Federal Decree-Law No. 9 of 2022 and its executive regulations (Cabinet Resolution No. 106 of 2022).
  • Recruit through a MOHRE-licensed recruitment office, such as a Tadbeer centre. The employer pays all recruitment and government fees — workers cannot be charged.
  • Workers are entitled to a paid weekly rest day, at least 12 hours' daily rest including 8 consecutive hours, 30 days' paid annual leave and a ticket home every two years.
  • Wages are due monthly and must be paid within 10 days of the due date. The Wage Protection System is mandatory for some domestic roles, including home caregivers.
  • End-of-service gratuity for domestic workers is legally unsettled — see section 6.

1. The three ways to hire

Tadbeer centres are MOHRE-licensed service centres run by private franchisees. They recruit workers, arrange visas, medicals and Emirates IDs, offer training and accommodation, and help resolve disputes. Most offer three kinds of arrangement:

  • Traditional sponsorship. You sponsor the worker on a two-year visa. You pay the recruitment package up front and the salary each month. A six-month probation period comes with replacement or refund terms.
  • Temporary or centre-sponsored. The centre holds the visa and you pay a monthly fee that bundles salary and costs. Faster to start — sometimes within a day — and easier to end.
  • Flexible. Hourly, daily, weekly or monthly help supplied by the centre, often live-out. Highest monthly cost, least paperwork.

You can also hire a worker already in the UAE through a legal transfer. Always check that the office you use holds a MOHRE licence; licensed centres display their licence number.

3. The worker's rights under the 2022 law

  • Weekly rest: one paid day off a week.
  • Daily rest: at least 12 hours a day, including at least 8 consecutive hours.
  • Annual leave: at least 30 days' paid leave a year; for service between six and twelve months, two days per month. Unused leave is paid out.
  • Travel home: a return ticket every two years.
  • Sick leave: up to 30 days a year, commonly the first 15 at full pay and the next 15 at half pay.
  • Documents: the worker keeps their own passport and identity documents.
  • Fair treatment: food, suitable accommodation, medical care and protection from abuse.

4. Paying wages and the WPS

Salary is paid monthly in dirhams and must be paid within 10 days of the due date. Since 2025 the Wage Protection System (WPS) is mandatory for five roles hired under domestic worker visas — private trainers, private teachers, home caregivers, private representatives and private agricultural engineers — and optional for maids, nannies, cooks and drivers. Even where it's optional, paying through WPS or a bank transfer gives both of you a clear record.

5. Probation, replacement and refunds

The law sets a six-month probation period. If the worker is found medically unfit or leaves during probation, the recruitment office must refund the full fee, including government fees, or provide a replacement on the terms agreed. After probation, refunds are typically pro-rata to the months remaining on the contract. Read the centre's replacement terms before you pay and keep them in writing.

6. End-of-service gratuity: the honest answer

This is the most misunderstood point. The old 2017 domestic worker law gave 14 days' wage for each year of service after one year. The 2022 law leaves end-of-service benefits to be set by Cabinet decision, and legal commentators disagree about what applies today: some report MOHRE guidance that 14 days per year still applies, while others say there is currently no statutory entitlement. The 21-day formula for private-sector employees does not apply to domestic workers.

Our recommendation: write a gratuity of at least 14 days' basic wage per year of service into your agreement. It's fair, it matches what many employers already pay, and it removes doubt. Use our gratuity calculator.

7. Transfers and ending the contract

Ending a contract, transferring a worker to a new employer or cancelling a visa goes through MOHRE and your Tadbeer centre. Pay all wages, leave and agreed benefits on the last day, and provide the ticket home if the contract ends. Never report a worker as "absconding" to avoid paying what's due — it is unfair and can carry penalties.

8. Day to day: what good employers do

  • Give a private room with a lock and air-conditioning, and decent food.
  • Agree a weekly schedule, including breaks and the day off.
  • Let the worker keep their phone and stay in touch with family.
  • Pay on the same day each month and keep a record.
  • Sign the Fair Home pledge.
