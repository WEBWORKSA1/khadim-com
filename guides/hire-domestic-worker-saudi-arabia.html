Hiring a domestic worker in Saudi Arabia

Musaned has made recruitment, contracts, transfers and salary payment digital. Here's how the process works in 2026 and what you owe the person you hire.

By the Khadim editorial team. Last checked 29 October 2026. 3 min read.

Key facts

  • Musaned is the official domestic labour platform of the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (HRSD).
  • Since 1 January 2026, all domestic workers' salaries must be paid electronically through Musaned-linked wallets or bank accounts.
  • Workers are entitled to 8 hours' unbroken daily rest, a weekly rest day, a month's paid leave after two years, a ticket home every two years and up to 30 days' sick leave.
  • HRSD guidance gives an end-of-service award of one month's wage after four consecutive years of service.
  • Employers may not charge workers for recruitment, permits, iqama or transfers, or keep their passports.

1. How recruitment works on Musaned

Musaned brings the whole process into one government platform: applying for a domestic worker visa, choosing a licensed recruitment office, signing the employment contract, tracking the worker's arrival and, later, transferring the worker to another employer. You log in with your national or residence identity. The platform also lists licensed recruitment offices with their service levels, so you can compare before you choose.

  1. Check eligibility and apply for a visa through Musaned and Absher. Government fees apply at this step.
  2. Choose a licensed recruitment office and the worker profile that fits, specifying the role, experience and languages.
  3. Sign the contract on Musaned. It records pay, duties and terms, and links to salary payment.
  4. Receive the worker, complete the medical check and residence permit, and set up electronic salary payment.

Recruitment office fees vary widely by the worker's country of origin and the office. Compare total costs, delivery time and replacement terms on Musaned before committing.

2. Hiring someone already in the Kingdom

Since 2023 a domestic worker can transfer to a new employer through Musaned, provided the current employer, the new employer and the worker all agree. Transfers are usually faster and cheaper than new recruitment, and you can meet the person first. Make sure there are no outstanding wages or disputes before you accept a transfer.

3. Pay: electronic salaries are now compulsory

The move to electronic wages was phased in from July 2024 and became mandatory for all domestic workers on 1 January 2026. Salaries are paid through digital wallets or banks linked to Musaned, and workers without a bank account can withdraw cash with a Mada card. The record protects both sides: the worker can prove they were paid, and you can prove you paid.

4. Rest, leave and working conditions

  • Daily rest: at least 8 continuous hours.
  • Weekly rest: one day a week.
  • Annual leave: one month's paid leave after two years of service.
  • Travel: a ticket home every two years.
  • Sick leave: up to 30 days a year.
  • Documents: the worker keeps their own passport.

5. End of service

HRSD's domestic worker rights guide provides an end-of-service award of one month's wage after four consecutive years of service. Rules were updated in 2025, so check the current text on HRSD or Musaned when a contract ends. Pay all wages and leave owed on the final day.

6. What employers must never do

Charging a worker for recruitment, visas, the residence permit or a transfer is prohibited, as is confiscating a passport. Penalties reported for violations include fines of up to SAR 20,000 and bans on recruiting for up to three years, with heavier penalties for repeat offences. Beyond the law, these practices drive good workers away and damage trust.

7. Good practice

  • Agree a written daily schedule with breaks and the rest day.
  • Provide a private room, proper food and medical care.
  • Let the worker keep their phone and contact family.
  • Pay on the same day every month, in full.
  • Take the Fair Home pledge.
