Live-in, full-time or part-time help?

The arrangement you choose shapes your costs, your privacy and your responsibilities as much as the person you hire. Here's how the three options compare.

By the Khadim editorial team. Last checked 29 October 2026. 3 min read.

Key facts

  • Part-time help is the most flexible and usually the cheapest for small homes.
  • Full-time live-out help gives one reliable person for most of the day, with clear boundaries.
  • Live-in help suits round-the-clock needs but brings legal duties for rest, food and accommodation.
  • In Singapore and Hong Kong, migrant domestic workers must live in; in the Gulf it's the norm.

When part-time is right

If you need a clean home and cooked meals but you're home in the evenings, two or three part-time helpers covering specific tasks are often the most efficient choice, especially in Indian cities where per-task pay is the norm. The trade-off: more people to coordinate, and no cover when someone is away.

When full-time live-out is right

If you need one person to run the home or care for children during the working day, a full-time live-out helper gives continuity without sharing your home. Pay covers more hours, so expect a higher monthly figure than part-time, and agree travel arrangements.

When live-in is right

Newborns, older parents who need help at night, very long working days and homes far from where workers live all make live-in help sensible. Being a good live-in employer means a private room with a lock, proper food, a clear end to the working day, unbroken sleep, a weekly day off and paid leave. Many live-in arrangements fail not on pay but on rest and privacy.

A quick way to decide

  1. Do you need help before 7am, after 8pm or at night? If yes, consider live-in or shift carers.
  2. Do you need one person for most of the day? If yes, full-time live-out.
  3. Do you need specific tasks done well, at set times? Part-time.
  4. Can you offer a private room and protected rest? If not, avoid live-in.
