Hiring a helper in Singapore

Singapore's Ministry of Manpower sets clear rules for employing a migrant domestic worker. Here's what you'll pay, what you must provide, and how to be an employer good helpers want to work for.

By the Khadim editorial team. Last checked 29 October 2026. 3 min read.

Key facts

  • Monthly levy: S$300 for the first helper and S$450 for each additional helper, or S$60 concessionary for eligible households with a young child, an elderly person or a person with disability.
  • A S$5,000 security bond is required for each helper (except Malaysians) and cannot be charged to the worker.
  • Medical insurance must cover at least S$60,000 a year, plus personal accident cover of at least S$60,000.
  • First-time helpers must attend the one-day Settling-In Programme within 7 days of arrival, before they start work.
  • Helpers get a weekly rest day, and at least one rest day a month cannot be replaced with pay.

1. Before you hire

You'll need to meet MOM's employer eligibility rules, choose between hiring through a licensed employment agency or directly, and decide whether you want a helper new to Singapore or a transfer helper already working here. Transfer helpers usually start faster and you can interview them in person. Agencies handle the work permit application, medical check and documentation for a fee; ask for the fee and replacement terms in writing.

Helpers must live at the employer's registered address. Plan a private space with proper ventilation and a mattress, and privacy for the helper.

Singapore has no minimum wage for helpers, but some origin countries set minimum salaries for their nationals, and experience pushes pay up. Use our pay planner and total cost calculator.

3. Paying salary the right way

Pay salary at least monthly, no later than 7 days after the end of the salary period, preferably into the helper's own bank account. If you pay in cash, keep a salary record signed by both of you. Never hold the helper's money, bank book or ATM card, and only deduct what the law allows.

4. Rest days and wellbeing

Helpers are entitled to a weekly rest day. Employer and helper can agree to compensate some rest days with pay, but at least one rest day a month must be taken and cannot be paid off. A rest day can be taken as two half-days and deferred by up to one month by agreement. If a helper works on a rest day, pay at least one extra day's salary. Give adequate daily rest, too — long, unbroken hours are the most common cause of burnout and conflict.

5. The Settling-In Programme

Helpers coming to work in Singapore for the first time attend a one-day programme on safety, adapting to life in Singapore and their rights and responsibilities. It must be completed within 7 days of arrival (excluding Sundays and public holidays), and the helper can't start work until they've attended. The employer pays.

6. Insurance and medical care

Buy medical insurance with annual claim limits of at least S$60,000 and personal accident insurance of at least S$60,000. For larger medical bills, insurers and employers share costs under MOM's co-payment rules. Bring your helper for the regular medical examination required to renew the work permit.

7. Being an employer helpers recommend

  • Agree house rules and a weekly schedule on day one.
  • Let the helper keep their phone and their passport.
  • Provide nutritious food and a proper place to sleep.
  • Never deploy a helper outside your home or for work not allowed by the permit.
  • Sign the Fair Home pledge.
