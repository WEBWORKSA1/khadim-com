Domestic worker salary guide 2026

Typical monthly pay for household help in nine countries, the legal minimums that apply, and what pushes pay up or down — so you can set a fair offer the first time.

By the Khadim editorial team. Last checked 29 October 2026. 3 min read.

Key facts

  • Hong Kong's Minimum Allowable Wage is HK$5,220 a month for contracts signed from 3 October 2026, plus food or a HK$1,236 food allowance.
  • Qatar's minimum wage of QAR 1,000 a month covers domestic workers, with food and housing on top.
  • Singapore, the UAE and Saudi Arabia have no general minimum wage for domestic workers; market rates and origin-country minimums apply.
  • In India, several states set minimum wages for domestic work, but city market rates are usually higher.
  • Combined roles — such as housekeeper-cook — typically pay 10–35% more than a single role.

What pushes pay up

  • More duties. Each extra responsibility — cooking on top of cleaning, or a second child — adds to pay.
  • Specialist skills. Newborn care, dementia care, special-needs childcare, specific cuisines and languages.
  • Experience and references. Years of steady work with good references are worth paying for.
  • Hours. Early starts, late finishes, nights and weekend work.
  • Location. Expensive neighbourhoods and long commutes.
  • Live-out vs live-in. In India and the West, live-out full-time work usually pays more cash; live-in includes food and housing.

Beyond salary: the full cost

Employers also pay for food and accommodation (live-in), insurance, visa and recruitment fees in the Gulf, Singapore and Hong Kong, levies in Singapore, payroll taxes in the UK, US and Canada, and bonuses and leave cover everywhere. Our total cost calculator adds it up.

How we estimate pay

We combine published agency rate cards and packages, current job listings, government minimums and reports from households and workers for each country. We take the middle of the market — excluding unusually low or high outliers — and round to typical ranges. India figures are for metro cities; we apply a discount for smaller cities. We review figures every year and whenever a minimum wage changes.

These are estimates for budgeting, not quotes or legal advice. Pay that is lower than the range can make it hard to find and keep good help; pay at or above the range attracts more experienced people.
