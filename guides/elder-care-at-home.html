Elder care at home for parents in India

Most older Indians want to stay in their own homes. This guide helps families — especially those living in another city or country — choose the right carer, budget honestly and keep watch from afar.

By the Khadim editorial team. Last checked 29 October 2026. 3 min read.

Key facts

  • A trained attendant handles daily living — bathing, dressing, walking, meals and medicines on time. A nurse handles clinical tasks such as injections and wound care.
  • Typical metro pay in 2026: full-time attendant ₹16,000–₹28,000 a month; qualified home nurses cost more.
  • Falls are the most common avoidable emergency. Grab bars, night lights and clear floors prevent many of them.
  • Plan cover for your carer's days off and leave before you need it.
  • India's national helpline for senior citizens, Elderline, is 14567.

1. Work out what care your parent actually needs

Write down a normal day: when they wake, wash, eat, take medicines, walk, rest and sleep, and where they struggle. Then sort needs into three groups:

  • Company and household help: cooking, cleaning, shopping, accompanying them to appointments.
  • Personal care: help with bathing, toileting, dressing, moving safely, feeding.
  • Clinical care: injections, wound or catheter care, feeding tubes, monitoring after surgery.

The first two are the work of an attendant or caregiver. The third needs a qualified nurse, at least for those tasks. Ask your parent's doctor which group they fall into, and review every few months — needs change.

3. What it costs in 2026

Indicative monthly pay in metro cities; smaller cities are typically 20–40% lower.

  • Day attendant (8–10 hours): ₹16,000 – ₹24,000
  • Live-in attendant: ₹18,000 – ₹28,000 plus food and a room
  • Night attendant (12 hours): ₹15,000 – ₹25,000
  • Qualified home nurse: noticeably higher, depending on qualifications and tasks

Two carers working shifts are often safer than one carer awake day and night. Budget for days off, festival bonus and leave cover. Our pay planner includes elder care.

4. Make the home safe

  • Grab bars beside the toilet and in the shower; a shower chair.
  • Non-slip mats; remove loose rugs and trailing wires.
  • Night lights between bedroom and bathroom.
  • A bed at the right height, with a rail if needed.
  • A list of emergency numbers on the fridge and in the carer's phone.
  • A weekly pill organiser, and a written medicine schedule.

5. Choosing and briefing the carer

Ask candidates about the specific needs your parent has — dementia, diabetes, mobility, recovery after a stroke. Ask how they'd help someone stand from a chair, what they'd do if your parent fell, and how they'd handle refusal to eat or take medicines. Do a paid trial while a family member is present.

On day one, give the carer a written care plan: routines, food preferences, medicines with times, doctor's numbers, what to do in an emergency, and who to call first. Ask them to keep a simple daily log.

6. Staying involved from abroad

  • A fixed daily call with your parent, and a short weekly call with the carer.
  • A shared log — a notebook photographed weekly, or a shared note on the phone — recording meals, medicines, sleep, mood and anything unusual.
  • A local anchor: a relative, neighbour or friend who visits weekly and is the first call in an emergency.
  • Money set up properly: pay the carer by bank transfer on a fixed date, and give them a small, accounted float for household purchases.
  • Visit or video-walk the home regularly to see how things really are.

7. Dementia and difficult days

Dementia care needs patience and specific skills: calm redirection, simple choices, routines, and watching for wandering. Choose a carer with real dementia experience and give them breaks — dementia care is exhausting. Ask your parent's doctor about local memory clinics and support groups.

8. Look after the carer, too

Carer burnout leads to mistakes and sudden resignations. Protect the weekly day off and nightly sleep, arrange cover, pay on time and say thank you. Consider nominating a great carer for our Home Heroes Awards.

Where to get help

Elderline (14567) is India's national helpline for senior citizens, offering information, guidance and help with abuse cases. For medical emergencies, call 112 or your local ambulance number.
