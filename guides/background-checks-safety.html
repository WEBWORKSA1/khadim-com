Background checks, references and home safety

Careful checks protect your family and the person you hire. Here's how to do them properly — legally, respectfully and without treating anyone as a suspect.

By the Khadim editorial team. Last checked 29 October 2026. 3 min read.

Key facts

  • Always get the candidate's consent before any background or police check.
  • In India, city police offer domestic help verification — Delhi Police, for example, through its online citizen services portal. It is usually voluntary.
  • In the UK, US and Canada, employers must check the legal right to work.
  • Keep copies of identity documents securely, only as long as needed, and never keep originals.

1. Identity

Ask to see original identity documents and keep a copy of what you need for your records. In India, a masked Aadhaar copy shows only the last four digits and is enough for most household purposes. Match the photo to the person and note the address. Never keep original documents.

2. References that tell you something

Call previous employers yourself, rather than relying on written letters. Good questions:

  • "What were their main duties, and how long did they work for you?"
  • "What were they best at? What would you have liked them to do differently?"
  • "Was there ever a situation that worried you?"
  • "Why did they leave? Would you hire them again?"

Hesitation on the last question tells you more than a glowing letter.

3. Police verification in India

Most city police forces run a tenant and domestic help verification service. In Delhi, you can register domestic help through the police's online citizen services; in many other cities you submit a form at the local police station or online with the worker's photo, identity proof and permanent address. You receive an acknowledgement, and the police verify records. It is usually voluntary, but strongly recommended — and agencies in some cities need it to be licensed.

Tell the worker why you're doing it, and that it's routine for everyone you hire.

4. Right-to-work and visas abroad

  • UK: check the right to work before employment starts.
  • US: complete Form I-9 to verify identity and work authorisation.
  • Canada: confirm the worker can legally work in Canada; foreign caregivers come through federal caregiver programs.
  • UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Singapore, Hong Kong: hire only through the official visa and permit route — never employ someone on another employer's visa without a legal transfer.

5. Cameras: where the line is

Cameras in shared areas are common. To use them fairly: tell the worker in writing before they start, never place cameras in their private room or any bathroom, avoid recording audio where the law restricts it, and keep footage secure. Check privacy and data-protection law in your country.

6. Simple safety rules for the home

  • A list of emergency numbers on the fridge and in the worker's phone.
  • A first-aid kit, and someone trained to use it.
  • Clear rules about visitors, deliveries and opening the door.
  • Medicines and cleaning chemicals out of children's reach.
  • A small, recorded float for household purchases, rather than open access to cash.
  • Agreed rules for the children: where they can go, screen time, food.

7. Safety works both ways

Workers face risks too — long hours, isolation, injury and abuse. A safe home for a worker means a private room with a lock, protected rest, freedom to leave on their day off, their own phone and documents, and someone to go to if there's a problem.

8. Protect the worker's data

Store copies of ID, verification receipts and references securely. Don't share them on messaging groups. Delete or return them when the job ends unless the law requires you to keep records.
