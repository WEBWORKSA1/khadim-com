Which kind of help do you actually need?

Most hiring problems start with the wrong job title. Here is what each role covers, the skills worth paying for, and where one role ends and another begins.

Housekeeper (maid, house help)

A housekeeper keeps the home clean and running: sweeping and mopping, dusting, bathrooms, dishes, laundry and ironing, changing beds and tidying. In India a part-time housekeeper often covers one or two tasks per visit and is paid per task; a full-time housekeeper covers the whole home on a fixed schedule. In the Gulf, Singapore and Hong Kong, a full-time housemaid usually lives in.

Worth paying more for: experience in homes like yours, care with delicate surfaces and fabrics, reliability over years, and the ability to plan the week without being told.

Not their job unless agreed: childcare, elder personal care and cooking. If you add these, you are hiring an all-round helper and pay should reflect it.

Cook

A cook plans and prepares family meals — breakfast, packed lunches, dinner — and keeps the kitchen clean afterwards. Part-time cooks in Indian cities typically come for a fixed slot once or twice a day and are paid by the number of people and meals. Good cooks are asked about cuisines, special diets (diabetic, low-salt, Jain, vegan), hygiene and portion planning.

Always ask for a trial meal and agree who buys groceries. If the cook also shops, agree a simple written expense log.

Nanny

A nanny cares for children in your home: feeding, naps, play, outings, school pickups, homework and bedtime routines. Nannies differ from babysitters, who cover occasional evenings. A nanny should know safe sleep for infants, basic paediatric first aid and how to calm a distressed child.

Ask for references from other parents and a paid trial day. See our full guide to hiring a nanny.

Newborn-care specialist (japa, postnatal carer)

A newborn-care specialist supports the mother and baby in the first weeks after birth: baby bathing and massage, burping, night feeds support, laundry for the baby, and help with the mother's rest and nutrition. In India this role is commonly called a japa maid and is usually live-in for one to three months.

Insist on safe-sleep practice (baby on their back, on a firm flat surface, nothing loose in the cot) and ask your paediatrician before any traditional practice involving oils, swaddling or feeding.

Elder caregiver (attendant)

An elder caregiver helps an older person with daily living — bathing, dressing, toileting, eating, walking safely, medicines at the right time — and provides company. They are not nurses and should not give injections or manage wounds unless trained and permitted. For dementia, ask about experience with confusion, wandering and sundowning.

Families abroad arranging care for parents should read our elder care at home guide.

Home nurse

A home nurse is a qualified nurse who performs clinical tasks at home: injections, wound and catheter care, monitoring vital signs, managing feeding tubes and post-surgery recovery. Ask for proof of nursing registration with the relevant council and check it. Home nurses cost more than attendants because the work carries clinical responsibility.

Driver

A family driver handles school runs, commutes, errands and appointments, and looks after the car. Check the licence class, ask for a driving record where available, and agree hours carefully — waiting time is working time. In Singapore and Hong Kong, a foreign domestic helper generally cannot be deployed as a driver; families hire drivers separately.

Gardener

A gardener maintains lawns, beds, terraces and potted plants, handles seasonal planting and pruning, and advises on what will grow. Many work across several homes, a few hours a week each.

Pet carer

A pet carer walks, feeds and grooms pets, gives medicines and spots signs of illness. Agree what happens in an emergency and which vet to call.

Combining roles

Combined roles — a housekeeper-cook, or a nanny who also does the children's laundry — are common and sensible. Two rules keep them fair: write every duty into the agreement, and pay more as the list grows. Our planner adds roughly 12% per extra duty, which matches what experienced workers ask for.
